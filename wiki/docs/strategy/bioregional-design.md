# Bioregional Design

- Political borders are arbitrary liabilities that rarely align with the planet's natural systems.
- A bioregion is defined by watersheds, ecosystems, climate zones, geology and soil, and topography.
- Organizing along bioregional lines makes water a shared responsibility and aligns agriculture with the land, reducing chemical inputs and supply chain reliance.
- Be pragmatic about purity: with a changing planet, assisted migration of species from other bioregions can be the most resilient choice.
- Growth follows the land's carrying capacity; a [cadre](../cadres/what-is-a-cadre.md) that outgrows a watershed splits along natural seams.
- Trade within and between bioregions is purposeful and reciprocal, born of mutual benefit rather than extraction.
- Bioregionalism is an antidote to the poison of nationalism.
