# Resource-led Analysis

- Start with what's physical and economic: energy, food, water, minerals, manufacturing, logistics, capital, labor. Do this before listening to the story.
- Follow the resources: who has what, who needs what, what's scarce, what moves where.
- Read beliefs and rhetoric as downstream of resource position and incentive, not as independent drivers.
- It's a method, not a doctrine. No ideological loyalty, just "follow the resources and the incentives they create."
- Map the flows before judging the players: control, scarcity, cost, and what breaks first.
- Trace incentives and constraints rather than stated intentions. Treat official justifications as signals to audiences, not explanations.
- Think in feedback and path dependence, not good intentions.
- **Financial fragility:** When credit and paper claims outgrow what's actually produced, resource-led analysis flags the fragility before the correction. Idealist faith in self-correcting markets gets surprised every time.
- **Pressure and sanctions:** What a target produces, sells, and trades determines whether pressure bites or is absorbed. Idealism overestimates the power of isolation, condemnation, and shared values.
- **Resource dependence:** Whoever depends on another's energy, water, or food takes the shock when the tap turns. Resource-led analysis sees the scramble and the blow before it lands.
- **Climate commitments:** Emissions trajectories, feedbacks, and infrastructure lock-in predict missed targets. Idealism over-promises on treaties and intentions.
- **Control of critical inputs:** Whoever controls the chips, minerals, and manufacturing holds the leverage. Resource-led analysis anticipates decoupling and export controls. Idealism's faith in interdependence is repeatedly caught out.
- Idealism confuses talk with motives.
- Idealism assumes institutions mean what they say.
- Idealism is prescriptive, describing how things should be rather than how they are.
- Idealism treats ideas as autonomous instead of as expressions of resource position and interest.
- Idealism ignores constraints: physics, energy budgets, capital, logistics.
- When values and incentives collide, incentives win.
- Resource-led analysis is probabilistic, not deterministic. It estimates the range of likely outcomes.
- It under-explains morale, identity, and meaning. Pair it with local knowledge.
- It's not a license for cynicism. Beliefs do shape action, but they form under material conditions.
- Consistent with a [realistic, pessimistic outlook](../philosophy/realistic-pessimism.md) and the [commitment to reality](../cadres/militant-scientists.md).
- Use it for risk assessment, resource planning, and refusing to be surprised by the world.
